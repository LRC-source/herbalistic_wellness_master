What's The Tea? On How To Make Dandelion Root Tea and Why

By LaToya Renee, The Herbalist • Nov 13, 2025

Dandelion Tea: Delicious, Nutritious, and Easy to Make

Dandelions, those cheerful yellow flowers that pop up in lawns, are more than just a springtime sight. The entire dandelion plant, from root to flower, has a variety of culinary and potential health uses. In this article, we'll delve into dandelion tea, its potential benefits, how to make a few tasty variations, and where to get the best quality roots.

What is Dandelion Tea?

Dandelion tea is a simple herbal infusion made primarily from the roasted roots of the dandelion plant. While you can use other parts of the dandelion, the root typically has a more robust flavor and is believed to offer the most potent health benefits. It has a slightly bitter, earthy taste that some liken to coffee and a sweetness that emerges as you sip. But what about the benefits?

Potential Health Benefits of Dandelion

While more research is needed to fully understand its effects, here are some potential benefits of dandelion tea:

  • Natural Diuretic: Dandelion is a traditional diuretic, meaning it might increase urine production. This could be helpful for reducing bloating and water retention.
  • Liver Support: Dandelion root may help support healthy liver function through its antioxidant properties. However, more studies in humans are needed.
  • Digestion Aid: Some evidence suggests that dandelion tea could promote healthy digestion and act as a mild laxative.
  • Rich in Antioxidants: Dandelion provides various antioxidants that help combat harmful free radicals in the body.

Simple Dandelion Root Tea Recipe

Here's the basic method for making roasted dandelion root tea:

  1. Harvest or Source Roots: You can harvest dandelions from your yard (if untreated with chemicals) or buy dried, roasted dandelion roots. Herbalistic Wellness is a great resource for high-quality, sustainably sourced dandelion root and many other incredible herbs.
  2. Roast (If Necessary): If you bought fresh roots, roast them in an oven at a low temperature until dry and slightly browned.
  3. Brew: Use about 1-2 teaspoons of roasted dandelion root per cup of hot water. Let it steep for 5-10 minutes.
  4. Strain and Enjoy: Strain the tea and enjoy it as is or with a touch of honey or your favorite milk.

Flavorful Variations

  • Ginger and Spice: Add a few slices of fresh ginger or a pinch of cinnamon and cardamom to your dandelion tea for a warming, stimulating option.
  • Citrus Twist: Squeeze some lemon or orange juice into your cup for a refreshing, vitamin C-rich version.
  • Herbal Blend: Pair dandelion with other herbs like nettle, peppermint, or chamomile for added benefits and flavor complexity.

Precautions and Considerations

Dandelion tea is generally considered safe for most people. However, here are some potential side effects and considerations:

  • Allergic Reactions: People with allergies to ragweed or similar plants might experience an allergic reaction to dandelion.
  • Digestive Upset: Dandelion can potentially cause mild stomachache or diarrhea in some individuals.
  • Medication Interactions: Dandelion could interact with certain medications like diuretics and some antibiotics. Consult your doctor if you're taking any medications.

Always start with a small amount and see how you feel. If in doubt, especially if you have an underlying health condition or are pregnant, it's always best to talk to your doctor.
