The Ashwagandha Leaf Ban 

By LaToya Renee, The Herbalist • Feb 25, 2024

What Holistic Wellness Seekers Need to Know

A Personal Note from LaToya Renee, The Herbalist The Herbalist: Navigating Herb News

Hey Herbie's. As a mom and an herbalist for years, I know how confusing and unsettling it can be when a beloved, powerful herb makes headlines for the wrong reasons. Ashwagandha (Withania Somnifera)—one of the foundational adaptogens we cherish for stress management and nervous system support—is currently at the center of a major regulatory update in India. My commitment at Herbalistic Wellness is always to guide you toward safe, effective, and ancestral plant medicine, which means we must talk about the distinction between traditional practices and modern supplement manufacturing.

India’s Crackdown: Understanding the Ashwagandha Leaf Ban

The news coming out of India is important for everyone involved in plant-based wellness: the country’s food regulator, the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), has cracked down on ashwagandha products. They have explicitly banned the use of ashwagandha leaves and their extracts in nutraceuticals and supplements, citing health and safety concerns. This regulatory move is designed to tighten oversight of key ingredients in popular wellness supplements.

Roots Only: The Traditional Mandate

What does this ban mean for us? Crucially, the FSSAI has confirmed that only the roots of the Ashwagandha plant are permitted for use in supplements. This aligns perfectly with age-old Ayurvedic medicine, where the root has been the primary source of medicinal benefit for over 3,000 years.

While ashwagandha leaves are sometimes incorporated by supplement companies, this recent ruling reinforces that the true power (the ability to reduce cortisol and enhance sleep) lies in the root extract.

The Ancestral Roots of Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha has been a cornerstone of Ayurvedic medicine for millennia. Known as a small evergreen shrub, its medicinal usage is deeply rooted in tradition. It is renowned as a rejuvenator used to promote longevity and restore balance. Historically, the root is the part of the plant that has been researched and celebrated for its adaptogenic properties. When we honor the plant, we honor the practices of our ancestors, who knew exactly which parts to use for maximum benefit and safety.

LaToya’s Herbalist Tip: Sourcing Wisdom

As your herbalist, my advice is simple: always know your source. If you are purchasing an ashwagandha product, insist on transparency. Ensure the label explicitly states that it uses root extract. If the ingredients list simply says "Ashwagandha (Withania Somnifera)" without specifying the plant part, it’s time to ask questions or seek an alternative from a supplier you trust. We must stick to the methods that have sustained our ancestors—using the powerful root for deep, effective healing.

The regulatory landscape in the USA concerning Ashwagandha as a dietary supplement remains less restrictive than India's explicit FSSAI ban. Since no government body in the USA has issued a widespread prohibition on the leaf or its extracts, it reinforces the crucial need for consumers to follow the principles of self-sourcing wisdom. In a less-restrictive market, the burden of ensuring a supplement contains only the traditionally-used and scientifically-backed root extract falls squarely on the individual, making label reading and supplier transparency absolutely essential for safety and efficacy.

Key Takeaways
  • India’s FSSAI has banned ashwagandha leaves and their extracts in supplements due to safety concerns.
  • Only the root of Ashwagandha is permitted for use in nutraceuticals and supplements.
  • This ban reinforces traditional Ayurvedic wisdom, which focuses on the root for its potent adaptogenic benefits.
  • When sourcing supplements, verify they contain 100% ashwagandha root extract for safety and efficacy.
