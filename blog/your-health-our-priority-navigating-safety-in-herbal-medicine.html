Your Health, Our Priority: Navigating Safety in Herbal Medicine

By LaToya Renee, The Herbalist • Mar 17, 2024
An Herbalist Point of View

Welcome back to "What's The Tea?"! I love exploring the incredible benefits of herbal remedies, from soothing teas to potent tinctures. However, alongside the exciting world of natural wellness comes a crucial conversation: safety in herbal medicine.

The phrase "natural is always safe" is a common misconception that can lead to unintended consequences. Just like pharmaceutical drugs, herbs contain powerful active compounds that can interact with your body, other medications, or existing health conditions. Understanding proper dosages, potential interactions, and essential precautions is paramount for a safe and effective herbal journey. Let's learn how to use herbs wisely and responsibly.

"Natural" Doesn't Mean "Risk-Free"

Herbal medicines have been used for centuries, and many are considered gentle. However, "gentle" doesn't equate to "inert." Plants produce complex chemicals to protect themselves and interact with their environment, and these very chemicals are what give them their medicinal properties. When consumed, these compounds can exert significant pharmacological effects within the human body.

The Golden Rule: Consult a Healthcare Professional

Before starting any new herbal remedy, especially if you have a pre-existing medical condition,1 are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are taking any medications (prescription or over-the-counter), always consult with a qualified healthcare professional. This includes your doctor, pharmacist, or a certified herbalist. They can provide personalized advice based on your unique health profile.

Dosage is Key: More is NOT Better

One of the most critical aspects of herbal medicine safety is proper dosage. Even though dosages vary for medicinal use of herbs exceeding recommended doses can lead to side effects, toxicity, or even diminish an herb's therapeutic effect.

  • Follow Guidelines: Always adhere to the dosage instructions provided by a reputable supplier or a qualified practitioner. Dosage is different for every Herb.
  • Start Low, Go Slow: If you're new to an herb, begin with the lowest recommended dose and gradually increase if needed, while monitoring your body's response.
  • Understand Forms: Dosages vary greatly between fresh herbs, dried herbs, teas, tinctures, and standardized extracts. Be aware of the form you are using.

Navigating Herb-Drug Interactions: A Critical Focus

This is arguably the most significant area of concern in herbal medicine safety. Herbs can interact with conventional medications in various ways, potentially altering the drug's effectiveness or increasing the risk of adverse reactions.

Here are some well-documented examples of common interactions:

  • St. John's Wort (Hypericum perforatum): This popular herb for mood support is a notorious interactant. It can significantly reduce the effectiveness of numerous medications, including oral contraceptives, antidepressants (leading to serotonin syndrome risk), blood thinners (like warfarin), antiretroviral drugs, and immunosuppressants [1].
  • Ginkgo Biloba: Known for cognitive support, Ginkgo can inhibit blood clotting. It may increase the risk of bleeding when taken with anticoagulant (blood thinner) medications like warfarin, aspirin, or NSAIDs [1].
  • Ginseng (Panax ginseng): Can affect blood glucose levels and may interact with antidiabetic medications. It can also interact with blood thinners, potentially increasing bleeding risk [2].
  • Kava (Piper methysticum): Used for anxiety and relaxation, Kava can enhance the effects of sedative medications and is associated with potential liver toxicity, especially with prolonged use or in combination with other liver-affecting substances (like alcohol) [2].
  • Licorice Root (Glycyrrhiza glabra): Large amounts can cause potassium depletion, which is particularly dangerous for individuals taking diuretics or medications for heart conditions.

Tip of the day: Maintain a complete list of all medications (prescription and over-the-counter), supplements, and herbal remedies you are taking. Share this list with ALL your healthcare providers.

Precautions for Specific Populations and Conditions

Certain individuals and health conditions require extra caution:

  • Pregnancy & Breastfeeding: Many herbs are contraindicated during pregnancy and lactation due to potential risks to the fetus or infant. Always consult with a doctor or experienced herbalist.
  • Children & Elderly: These populations are often more sensitive to the effects of herbs and may require lower dosages.
  • Chronic Health Conditions: If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, autoimmune diseases, liver or kidney disease, or any other chronic condition, your body's response to herbs may be altered, and interactions with your condition's medications are more likely.
  • Before Surgery: Discontinue herbal remedies at least two weeks prior to any scheduled surgery, as some can affect blood clotting, blood pressure, or interact with anesthesia [2].

The Importance of Quality Sourcing and Purity

The herbal supplement market is not as strictly regulated as pharmaceutical drugs in many countries, leading to potential issues:

  • Contamination: Products can contain heavy metals, pesticides, or microbes.
  • Adulteration: Products might be mixed with cheaper, potentially harmful ingredients or even pharmaceutical drugs.
  • Misidentification: The wrong plant might be used, leading to ineffective or dangerous products.
  • Inconsistent Potency: The concentration of active compounds can vary widely [3].

Always purchase herbs and herbal products from reputable suppliers who provide information on sourcing, testing, and quality control. Look for third-party certifications where available.

When to Stop and Seek Medical Attention

If you experience any unusual or adverse reactions after taking an herbal remedy (e.g., severe headaches, allergic reactions, digestive upset, dizziness, changes in heart rate, etc.), discontinue use immediately and seek medical attention. Report serious adverse events to your healthcare provider.

Empowering Your Herbal Journey with Knowledge

Herbal medicine offers a wealth of potential benefits for wellness and health. By approaching its use with respect for mother nature, knowledge, and caution, you can safely integrate these potent plant allies into your life. Always remember: informed choices are the foundation of effective and safe herbal wellness.
