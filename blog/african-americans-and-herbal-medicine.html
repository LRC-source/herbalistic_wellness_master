African Americans and Herbal Medicine

By LaToya Renee, The Herbalist • May 15, 2026

Celebrating African Americans and Herbal Medicine During Black History Month

As February marks Black History Month, it's crucial to recognize the contributions of African Americans to herbal medicine. From discovering the healing properties of plants and herbs to developing treatments and remedies, African Americans have significantly influenced the evolution of herbal medicine. Let's explore some of the most influential figures in this field and their contributions.

Dr. George Washington Carver: Pioneer in Herbal Medicine

Dr. George Washington Carver was a scientist, educator, and inventor who transformed agriculture. Although he is best known for his work with peanuts, Carver was also an expert in herbal medicine. He studied the medicinal properties of plants and herbs, creating treatments and remedies that became widely used. Carver wrote extensively on herbal medicine and taught classes on using herbs and plants for healing.

Dr. Charles Henry Turner: Bridging Zoology and Herbal Medicine

Dr. Charles Henry Turner, a zoologist and entomologist, made groundbreaking discoveries about insect behavior. He was also an avid student of herbal medicine, writing extensively about the healing properties of plants and herbs. Turner's work brought herbal medicine into the mainstream, contributing to the development of treatments and remedies for various illnesses.

Dr. Rebecca Lee Crumpler: A Trailblazer in Medicine

Dr. Rebecca Lee Crumpler was the first African American woman to earn a medical degree in the United States. She was a dedicated student of herbal medicine, writing extensively about the healing properties of plants and herbs. Crumpler's efforts helped integrate herbal medicine into mainstream healthcare.

The Historical Significance of Herbal Medicine in African American Culture

Black history and herbal medicine are deeply intertwined. For centuries, African Americans have used plants, herbs, and other natural remedies to treat various ailments. From ancient Egyptians to enslaved Africans in the American South, herbal medicine has been a vital part of black history.

Important Considerations for Herbal Medicine

When exploring herbal medicine, it's essential to conduct thorough research. Not all herbs are safe for everyone, and understanding potential risks is crucial. Consulting with a qualified healthcare practitioner before starting any herbal treatment is advisable. Additionally, ensure you purchase herbs from reputable sources, as quality can vary significantly between brands.

Popular Herbs in African American Herbal Medicine

Several herbs are particularly popular among African Americans for their healing properties:

  • Ginger: Used to treat nausea, pain, and inflammation.
  • Turmeric: Known for its anti-inflammatory and pain-relieving properties.
  • Chamomile: Commonly used to treat anxiety and insomnia.

Other Beneficial Herbs

Apart from these popular herbs, many others can treat a wide range of ailments:

  • Dandelion: Used to treat digestive issues.
  • Echinacea: Known for boosting the immune system.
  • St. John’s Wort: Used to treat depression.

Conclusion

The field of herbal medicine offers a wealth of knowledge and endless benefits. African Americans have played a significant role in its development. By continuing to explore and appreciate their contributions, we honor their legacy and ensure the continued growth of herbal medicine.
