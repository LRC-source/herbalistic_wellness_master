# Home Extraction Ratios & Menstruum Calculations Made Simple
*By LaToya Renee, Master Herbalist*
*Date: August 29, 2026*

As a master herbalist, I get asked all the time: "LaToya, how do I make my home remedies feel as potent and consistent as what I buy from professional apothecaries?" The secret isn't in magic spells; it is in precise math. Today, I'm demystifying menstruum calculations, solvent ratios, and hydration offsets.

When we craft liquid extracts (tinctures), our primary goal is to pull active botanical constituents out of plant tissues and hold them in a stable solution. To do this consistently, we must understand extraction ratios and menstruum strength.

## Understanding the Weight-to-Volume Ratio

In modern Western herbalism, extractions are defined by a dry weight-to-volume ratio:
*   **Dry Botanical Extractions (1:5):** This means that for every 1 gram of dry plant material, you use 5 milliliters of liquid solvent (menstruum).
*   **Fresh Botanical Extractions (1:2):** Since fresh plants contain cellular water, we extract them at a tighter ratio of 1 gram of fresh plant to 2 milliliters of menstruum.

Let's look at how to calculate these values step by step.

## Step-by-Step Menstruum Calculation

## The Hydration Offset for Fresh Herbs

When working with fresh plants, they bring their own water to the extraction process. If you ignore this, your alcohol level will drop too low, causing your extract to spoil or cloud. To offset this, we calculate the moisture weight of the fresh herb and subtract it from the water portion of our menstruum calculation.
